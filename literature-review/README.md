# Literature Review

Research papers, notes, and summaries related to AI-supported assessment verification.
 PAPER-1
 
 Generative AI and Assessment in Higher Education
 
 LINK: https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s41239-024-00468-z?utm_source

 This paper examines the growing impact of generative artificial intelligence on assessment practices in higher education. Through a review of existing studies, the authors found that generative AI tools challenge traditional assessment methods because students can now produce high-quality assignments with AI assistance. The review highlights concerns regarding academic integrity, authenticity of student work, and the reliability of conventional grading approaches. At the same time, the paper identifies opportunities for educators to redesign assessments that emphasize critical thinking, reflection, and demonstration of understanding rather than simple content production. The authors conclude that higher education institutions should move away from assessment models that focus solely on final artifacts and instead adopt approaches that verify student learning and engagement throughout the learning process. These findings support the motivation behind VIVA-AI, which seeks to verify student understanding through individualized post-submission assessments.


 PAPER-2
 
 Evaluating an online assessment framework through the lens of Generative AI
 
 LINK: https://open-publishing.org/publications/index.php/APUB/article/view/1423?utm_source
 
 This paper argues against relying on AI detection tools as a primary strategy for maintaining academic integrity in higher education. The author demonstrates that AI detectors frequently produce inaccurate results, including false positives and false negatives, making them unreliable for high-stakes educational decisions. The paper emphasizes that attempts to identify whether AI was used are becoming increasingly difficult as generative AI systems improve. Rather than focusing on detection, the author recommends shifting toward assessment practices that directly evaluate student understanding and learning outcomes. The paper concludes that educational institutions should prioritize assessment verification methods over AI detection technologies. This conclusion aligns closely with the VIVA-AI framework, which does not attempt to detect AI use but instead verifies whether students understand the work they submit through personalized follow-up assessments.


 PAPER-3
 
 Contra generative AI detection in higher education assessments
 
 LINK: https://research.manchester.ac.uk/en/publications/contra-generative-ai-detection-in-higher-education-assessments-2/?utm_source

This study explores the use of oral assessments as a method for strengthening academic integrity and evaluating student understanding in data analytics education. The findings suggest that oral assessments provide instructors with a more direct and reliable way to determine whether students genuinely understand their submitted work. Students also reported that the process encouraged deeper learning and preparation. However, the study identifies a major limitation: oral assessments require significant instructor time and effort, making them difficult to implement in large classes. The authors conclude that while oral assessments are effective for verifying learning, scalability remains a challenge. This limitation provides a strong justification for VIVA-AI, which aims to capture many of the verification benefits of oral assessments while using AI-generated quizzes and Canvas automation to make the process practical for larger courses.
